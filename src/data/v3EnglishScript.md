ii.

Over darkness, the distant sound of children playing. Beneath it,
the frail murmur of an old man’s prayer.
SHEIKH ISA (O.S.)
Ya Allah... guide what remains of me.

INT. SHEIKH ISA’S PRAYER ROOM — DAY — 1932
A Qur’an rests open on a carved wooden stand. Prayer beads hang
from the wrist of aged hands cupped in prayer.
SHEIKH ISA (O.S.)
Forgive what was weak in me. Accept
what was done in Your name.
SHEIKH ISA BIN ALI AL-KHALIFA (80’s) kneels on a prayer mat near
the window. The sounds of children drift in from outside -
laughter, the bright chaos of life continuing.
SUPER: MANAMA/BAHRAIN — 1932
SHEIKH ISA
(CONT'D)
And before I return to You... let me
know whether I honoured the trust that
was given to me.
Footsteps approach.
NOURA (10) appears in the doorway behind Sheikh Isa. Bright-eyed,
caught between childhood and the first stirrings of duty. She
sidles inside, trying to hide.
Sheikh Isa lifts his weathered face, his eyes still sharp.
SHEIKH ISA
(CONT'D)
(teasing)
I know you are there, Noura.
Noura freezes, embarrassed.
NOURA
Sorry, Grandfather. We were playing
hide and seek.
SHEIKH ISA
Then you chose a poor hiding place. I
found you too easily.
A small smile. He gestures to her -
SHEIKH ISA
(CONT'D)
Come. Sit with me a while.

Noura enters and settles beside him. She notices the open Qur’an,
the prayer beads still resting in his hand.
NOURA
Were you asking Allah for something?
SHEIKH ISA
For guidance.
NOURA
You are Hakim.
SHEIKH ISA
That is why I need it most.
NOURA
Even now, Grandfather?
SHEIKH ISA
I am not so old, Noura, that I stopped
learning.
NOURA
Is that how you became so wise?
SHEIKH ISA
Wisdom comes with lessons learned.
(beat)
When I was your age, I thought a dream
was only something a man saw in his
sleep. I have learned the dreams that
change the world are different.
Noura listens with the solemn attention of a child sensing she
is being trusted with something important.
SHEIKH ISA
(CONT'D)
I’ve spent my life trying to
understand my father’s dream.
Trying to unravel its meaning.
NOURA
Is it like a puzzle?
Sheikh Isa grips the prayer beads tightly. His eyes mist.
SHEIKH ISA
I suppose it is.
NOURA
I’m good at puzzles.
He turns to her and smiles broadly.

SHEIKH ISA
Do you have time this morning to
sit with your grandfather a while?
NOURA
Off course. I’d like that very much.
SHEIKH ISA
Then let me tell you about
blessings and burdens - and lessons
learned.
Isa turns back toward the window. His old eyes drift past the
children, past the sunlight, into memory.
SHEIKH ISA
(CONT'D)
Maybe together we can solve the
puzzle of my father’s dream -

INT. YOUNG ISA’S BEDROOM - MORNING - 1861
The eyes of young ISA (13) open at the CALL OF A CRIER.
He’s slept in. He throws himself from the bed, pulls on his
clothes, splashes water on his face, rushes to the door, as -
Through the window -
MANAMA sprawls below. Smoke from ovens. Masts in the harbour. A
place made of labour, trade, tide and ambition.
SUPER: MANAMA/BAHRAIN - 1861

EXT. MANAMA - CONTINUOUS
MERCHANTS lifting shutters, dragging crates, counting yesterday’s
fortunes and today’s risks.
SHEIKH ISA (V.O.)
Before offices, before men arrived
with maps and treaties, Bahrain
listened to the sea. It fed us. It
tested us. It took from us. And
still our men returned to it.
BUILDERS chant in rhythm as they haul timber into place.
Boats rock at anchor. Nets are shaken out. Donkeys are led through
narrow lanes. Women draw water. Men work by the shore.
Bahrain feels alive — restless, practical, unsentimental.

Isa on horseback rides at breakneck speed through the waking town.
SHEIKH ISA (V.O.)
The nakhuda knew the stars. The diver
knew the dark beneath the water. The
women knew the sound of waiting.
Isa rides past a BLACKSMITH clanging metal. Past CARPENTERS
carrying beams on their shoulders. Past MERCHANTS arguing
cheerfully. Past DATE SELLERS arranging their stock.
The town belongs to Isa, and he belongs to it.
SHEIKH ISA (V.O.)
And I, born to the desert, believed I
knew. Land, people, blood, faith - we
are told to protect these. But no one
tells us how both burdens and
blessings can be part of the same
journey.
The twin minarets of AL-KHAMIS MOSQUE rise in the distance.

EXT. ROYAL RESIDENCE - CONTINUOUS
As Isa rides past the gates of the palace, HAYA (12) daughter of
the ruler, runs out from the courtyard.
HAYA
Isa! Wait!
He pulls up, smiling, as she hurries to his side.
HAYA (CONT'D)
Dates. For the breakfast you clearly
never ever eat.
She hands him a small cloth bundle. He takes it. For a moment,
they simply look at one another.
ISA
Thank you, Haya.
HAYA
Why are you always in such a rush?
Isa laughs, already turning his horse.
ISA
Because life will not slow down for me
to catch it.

He spurs forward. Haya watches him ride away, smiling —
admiration, and something more.

EXT. COASTAL ROAD — MOMENTS LATER
Three BAHRAINI BOYS ride toward the harbour. Isa effortlessly
sweeps up beside them.
ISA
You ride like old men on turtles!
He rockets ahead, as the boys roar with laughter and give chase.

EXT. PEARLING VILLAGE / DOCK – CONTINUOUS
On the outskirts of Manama, a small village by the water - modest
in its dwellings, rich in pearls. Dhows ghost through the morning
mist. Pearl divers haul ropes along the dock.
Isa dismounts. Runs along the dock.
HASSAN (70s) an old nakhuda — a pearling sea captain — inspects
the rigging of his moored dhow, testing each knot.
ISA
Morning, Hassan. You greet the sea
before your own wife?
HASSAN
The sea complains less, Young Isa.
Isa climbs down a ladder to a humble rowboat tied below.
AHMED (15) his older brother, waits aboard. Steady where Isa is
restless. Composed where Isa is all spark and motion.
AHMED
Late again, little brother.
ISA
Only when you come early, Ahmed.
AHMED
I came with the tide. You came with
excuses. Where are the others?
A shout from the dock.
BOY
Here!

Above them - the other boys arrive breathless from the ride,
clutching ropes and baskets.
Isa beams.
ISA
See? Perfect timing.

EXT. ROWBOAT / NEAR COAST — LATER
An anchor is dropped.
The small rowboat bobs a hundred metres from shore, surrounded by
working dhows. Pearl divers vanish beneath the surface one by one,
ropes trailing after them into the blue.
The boys prepare to dive.
ISA
They say the pearling is good here.
AHMED
They say many things.
He dives in. A clean, fearless plunge.
BOY #1
What is he after?
AHMED
The black pearl.
BOY #2
That is only a legend.
AHMED
Tell that to Isa.
Ahmed sighs, then dives after his brother.

EXT. BELOW THE SURFACE - CONTINUOUS
Another world. Blue-green light. Coral. Fish scattering like
thrown jewels.
Isa is at home in the sea.
Above him, Ahmed and the others follow, less certain, less
graceful.
Isa goes deeper.

Sunlight spears through the water. Sound falls away. Only current.
Blood. Breath held against time.
Isa cuts lower, longer, deeper than he needs to — chasing the
seabed lower.
Ahmed sees this and gestures sharply: enough.
Isa ignores him.
He reaches the bottom, searching among shell and stone with fierce
concentration — trying to prove something, though perhaps even he
does not know what.
His hand brushes coral. Silt blooms. Then he sees it.
A shell wedged beneath rock.
Isa reaches for it. Fingers close around it. He pulls it free.

EXT. SEA SURFACE / ROWBOAT — MOMENTS LATER
Ahmed bursts up first. Then one boy. Then another. They wipe water
from their faces, gasping.
No Isa. A flicker of concern. Ahmed scans the water.
Then Isa explodes through the surface, gulping air, wild with
triumph. He holds up his prize. A shell.
The boys laugh with relief. They clamber back aboard their boat.
Isa works a knife into the shell, prying it open with exaggerated
care. The boys crowd around.
At last, he holds up the tiniest pearl imaginable. Barely a pearl.
AHMED
You nearly drowned for that?
BOY #1
It is not even a little black.
The boys laugh.
ISA
I shall find it one day. The sea can
not hide it forever.
He grins - impossible not to like.
Ahmed’s eyes drift past Isa. His smile fades.

A ship at anchor by the bend. Low. Fast. Sinister.
AHMED
Pirates.
The boys turn.
ON THE DOCK - Figures run. Screams carry faintly over the water.
SOMALI PIRATES HURL jars of naphtha — a crude local “fire oil”
made from pitch/bitumen, resin, and sulphur. The jars smash. A
pirate’s torch arcs through the air. Lands.
WHOOMPH. The oil erupts in flames.
Ignited oil spreads across the dock, the surface of the sea,
cutting Hassan’s dhow off, trapping it inside a ring of flame.
Pirates retreat with stolen goods toward their boats.
On the trapped dhow, Hassan chokes in smoke as the flames close
in.
Isa steps to the edge of the boat. Ahmed grabs him by the arm.
AHMED (CONT'D)
You cannot reach him through that.
ISA
Then I’ll go under it.
Isa tears free and dives.
AHMED
Isa!
Isa swims towards the burning dhow.

EXT. DOCK — SAME
BAHRAINI FIGHTERS charge into the chaos, swords drawn.
At their head is ALI BIN KHALIFA AL KHALIFA (40s) — father to
Ahmed and Isa. He cuts through the pirates with controlled
ferocity, then sees the nakhuda, the burning water. Sees Isa
swimming towards the flames.
ALI
Isa!
UNDERWATER

Isa drives down beneath the burning surface. Above him, the sea
is on fire — a living ceiling of flame, rippling orange and
black. He swims hard, eyes open against the sting, the heat
pressing down through the water.
Isa reaches the dhow - rises inside the ring of flame.
IBRAHIM (20’s) Ali’s eldest son, leads more riders onto the
flaming dock. With swift, brutal discipline, they fall upon
pirates who have not escaped.
ON THE DHOW - Hassan can barely breathe. He’s terrified.
Isa scrambles aboard. Flames lick closer across the oil-slick
water. The dock is fully ablaze.
Isa looks to shore. No path. Only fire.
ISA
Hold your breath.
The old man is frozen with fear. Isa grabs him.
ISA (CONT'D)
Now!
He hauls him over the side. They plunge beneath the flames.

EXT. UNDERWATER - CONTINUOUS
Isa drags Hassan beneath the burning slick.
Above them, fire rolls across the surface. No escape.
Isa kicks hard. Too hard.
The old man slips from his grasp.
Isa catches him again, but his strength is failing. His lungs
burn. His limbs slow. His body begins to convulse.
A stream of air escapes him.
Then — a shape cuts through the water.
A DIVING STONE tied to a trailing rope.
Isa grabs the rope with one hand, holds Hassan tight with the
other.

EXT. DOCK — CONTINUOUS
Ali stands at the far end of the rope, surrounded by his men.
ALI
Pull them!
Ali and the men haul together, dragging the rope hand over hand
like a tug of war.

EXT. UNDERWATER – CONTINUOUS
Isa clings to the rope, one arm locked around Hassan.
They’re dragged through water beneath flames - toward shore.

EXT. DOCK / SHORELINE – CONTINUOUS
Ali and his men strain, hauling the rope with all they have.
Isa and Hassan break through beyond the fire.
Men rush into the shallows and drag them from the sea.
Hassan collapses, coughing seawater.
Isa gulps air, badly shaken, trying not to show it.
Out beyond the headland, the pirate ship raises anchor. The raid
ends as quickly as it began.
Ali strides toward Isa on the beach.
First relief. Then fury.
ALI
What were you thinking, Isa? Or
where you thinking nothing at all!
ISA
I couldn’t leave him.
ALI
So you chose to give two lives to
the fire.
Beyond them, men stamp at the flames still eating the dock.
ALI
(CONT'D)
Courage without thought is
foolishness. Do you understand?

Isa lowers his eyes. He nods.
Ahmed and the other boys have landed their boat nearby. Ahmed
runs forward, relieved his brother is alive.
ALI
(CONT'D)
And you, Ahmed. You didn’t have the
sense to stop your brother?
Ahmed bows his head, ashamed.
Ibrahim rides up.
IBRAHIM
Father may not reach you next time,
Isa.
Insult to injury.
HASSAN
May Allah preserve the boy.
Ali glances at him, then back to Isa.
SHEIKH ALI
And preserve him from himself.
Ali spins on his heels and heads to the dock to help with
putting out the flames. Ibrahim follows. So does Ahmed.
Isa is left shaken. He knows how close he came to death.
SHIEKH ISA (V.O.)
A man was trapped.

INT. SHEIKH ISA’S PRAYER ROOM - DAY - 1932
SHEIKH ISA
I saw only his life. My father saw
mine as well. That was the first
valuable lesson he taught me.
Noura sits forward.
NOURA
Was that his dream? To be rid of the
pirates?
Isa smiles faintly.
SHEIKH ISA
Perhaps one piece of it.
(a beat)
(MORE)

SHEIKH ISA (CONT'D)
But a good puzzle is never so generous
as to give itself away at the
beginning.
Noura smiles. She is enjoying this immensely.
NOURA
Tell me more, Grandfather.
Isa shuts his eyes - remembering again.

INT. BRITISH OFFICE - DAY - 1861
A pen scratches across parchment.
SHEIKH MOHAMMED BIN KHALIFA (50s), ruler of Bahrain, Haya’s
father, signs the British agreement. Across the room, British
officers watch in stiff silence.
Behind Mohammed stand SHEIKH ALI, young ISA, and his brothers —
absorbing the moment without yet understanding its full weight.
SHEIKH ISA (V.O.)
I was still a boy when your great
grandfather, Sheikh Mohammed, signed
the agreement with the British. They
said it would protect our ships from
pirates and keep the Gulf safe.
The British officer takes the signed document. Young Isa watches
his uncle’s face. There is no triumph in it.
SHEIKH ISA (V.O.)
But sometimes, Noura, when powerful
men offer peace, they also ask you to
give something up. Your freedom. Your
ships. Your choices. I did not
understand that then.

INT. BRITISH OFFICE - DAY - 1868 - TIME CUT
The same room. Different British faces.
SHEIKH ALI (50s now) sits in the same chair once occupied by his
brother. He signs another declaration.
Behind him stand IBRAHIM (30s), AHMED (21), and ISA (19) — no
longer boys.
SUPER: BAHRAIN - 1868

SHEIKH ISA (V.O.)
Years passed. Relations between the
British and Sheikh Mohammed soured,
and he left for Zubarah. My father had
to sit in that same chair.
Ali finishes signing. The British officer gathers the paper.
SHEIKH ISA (V.O.)
They gave him a choice, but not the
kind a man wants. He signed not to be
made ruler, but to keep Bahrain safe
from the danger it would face from
every side.
Young Isa watches his father rise.

INT. ROYAL RESIDENCE / COURTYARD — DAY — 1868
Sheikh Ali, Ibrahim and Ahmed sit at a long table in the shaded
courtyard, relaxed and content.
SHEIKH ISA (V.O.)
When my father became ruler, the
lessons did not end. They became
harder.
Isa enters, dust on his clothes, energy still in him. He snatches
up an apple and bites into it.
AHMED
Isa. Last to arrive, first to eat.
ISA
Or you’d leave me with crumbs,
Ahmed.
The courtyard is alive with privilege and purpose.
Servants hold falcons and hawks, their wings flashing in the
sun. Tribal chiefs in vivid robes confer from high-backed
chairs. A SERVANT draws syrup from a madbasa date press.
Beside Ali sit his WIFE and adviser, SALEH, 30s. Nearby stands
MAJEED, 21, Ali’s assistant — almost invisible.
SALEH
The first harvest reports are strong,
Your Honour. The best in years.
Sheikh Ali nods, troubled.

Across the courtyard, Isa and Ahmed have already drawn practice
swords, circling one another. Isa moves with flourish. Ahmed with
easy amusement.
Isa knocks a serving dish from a passing servant’s tray. Majeed
catches it before it hits the ground. No one notices.
The boys beckon Ibrahim to join. Ibrahim looks to Sheikh Ali.
Ali smiles, giving permission.
Ibrahim rises and draws his sword.
A SALUKI noses after scraps beneath the table as the brothers move
into the open space.
Steel whispers, then rings.
Ahmed grins through the play. Ibrahim is composed and economical.
Isa attacks with flair — but too much force.
AHMED
There he is. Fighting the air harder
than the man.
Isa presses harder. Ibrahim turns aside each strike with
irritating ease.
IBRAHIM
Careful, Isa. Haste is not victory.
Ahmed sees the change first.
AHMED
Isa —
Isa lunges. The play gone. Steel cracks. Ibrahim gives ground now,
forced to work as Isa drives at him with real anger.
Isa strikes HARDER. The tip of his blade flashes past Ibrahim’s
face — close enough to draw a breath from everyone watching.
Suddenly, Sheikh Ali is between them, gripping Isa’s sword arm.
SHEIKH ALI
Enough.
Isa stands breathing hard. Ibrahim steps back, no longer smiling.
Ali takes the sword from Isa’s hand.

SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
The Prophet said the strong man is not
the one who overcomes others by force,
but the one who controls himself in
anger.
ISA
He provoked me.
SHEIKH ALI
If mockery can govern you, you are
already in another man’s hands.
That stings.
Ali offers the sword back, hilt first.
Isa takes the sword, chastened but listening, the lesson burning
deeper than the reprimand.

EXT. TREE OF LIFE - DUSK
A majestic FALCON glides across the darkening sky, riding the last
warm currents of the day.
Beneath the vast canopy of the TREE OF LIFE, SHEIKH ALI stands
with his three sons: IBRAHIM, AHMED, and ISA. The desert stretches
endlessly around them. Nothing should grow here, but this one tree
stands defiantly.
Ali places a hand against the tree’s ancient bark.
SHEIKH ALI
Look at this tree, my sons. It lives.
While nothing around it does. Its
roots reach where the eye cannot.
The boys listen. Ibrahim calm authority. Ahmed quiet attention.
Isa restless, eager to understand, but even more to be noticed.
SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
Bahrain is not only what we see.
He offers a water bladder to Ibrahim first. Ibrahim drinks, then
passes it to Ahmed. Ahmed drinks and passes it to Isa.
SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
The country provides. Even where the
eye sees only sand.
Isa drinks, watching his father.

SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
Fresh water springs beneath the waves.
Allah’s miracle. Two seas. One salt,
one fresh.
Isa passes the bladder back to his father.
SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
Since our ancestors founded Al-
Zubarah, we have known peace and
challenge. Wisdom secures allies.
Wealth invites rivals.
He pours a thin stream of water onto the soil beneath the Tree.
SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
This is why we build bridges, not
walls. Why we keep our alliances
strong.
Isa watches the water vanish instantly into the thirsty ground.
SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
Even when the British speak of peace
as though it were theirs to give.
Their treaties are written on shifting
sands.
The falcon descends and lands on Ibrahim’s arm. Natural. Almost
accidental. But Isa notices.
IBRAHIM
They say they restrain piracy. Yet
little by little, every fleet on this
coast grows smaller but their own.
AHMED
What is the answer, Father?
SHEIKH ALI
We strengthen our alliances.
Isa cannot hold back.
ISA
Why call them allies?
IBRAHIM
(cautioning)
Isa.
SHEIKH ALI
You speak as though courage and
patience are enemies.
(MORE)

SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
(a beat)
You must also learn to listen. That is
where wisdom begins.
The rebuke lands hard.
17.
SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
There is more to our land than pearls,
or how the British measure us. More
than fresh water beneath the sea.
Isa listens, not fully understanding, but feeling the weight in
his father’s voice.
SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
I believe it. I dream of it.
Ibrahim raises his arm. The falcon launches into the dusk.
SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
One day, Ibrahim, you will carry forth
that dream.
The words strike Isa harder than any reprimand.
Ibrahim bows his head.
IBRAHIM
It will be my duty and my honour,
Father.
Isa hides the hurt, watching the falcon fly into the distance.

EXT. ROYAL RESIDENCE / RAMPARTS - NIGHT
ISA stands alone. Restless. Troubled. The argument at the Tree
still burns in him.
The sky is a canopy of stars. In the distant harbour, a ship’s
lantern sways in the dark.
SHEIKH ALI (O.S.)
Bahrain is a ship, Isa.
Isa turns as his father approaches.
SHEIKH ALI
(CONT'D)
Some men pull the ropes. Some dive
beneath the water. Some watch the
stars. Some pray on the shore.
He places a hand on Isa’s shoulder.

SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
But if any one loses his nerve, all
of them drown.
ISA
You ask Ibrahim what he thinks. You
never ask me.
SHEIKH ALI
Ibrahim has his path. Ahmed has
his. And you - you shall play your
part in time.
The weight of what his father is to say sits heavily between them.
SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
You must study. Beyond our shores.
In England.
The words hit Isa like exile.
ISA
You are sending me away?
SHEIKH ALI
I am preparing you.
ISA
For what? To be useful somewhere
else?
For a moment, father and son face one another in the starlight.
SHEIKH ALI
The world is moving faster. The
British understand this. If we
remain only what we have been,
Bahrain will be swept aside.
ISA
I want to be here. I want to stand
beside you. If there is a fight,
then let me fight it with you.
SHEIKH ALI
Anger is a poor navigator, Isa.
Isa has no answer. Only hurt.
SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
The sea has fed us. Pearls are the
blessing we know. There are other
blessings to be found.
(MORE)

SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
The land has not yet spoken. When
it does, someone must know how to
listen.
He studies Isa’s face - his youth, his fire, his impatience.
SHEIKH ALI (CONT'D)
Isa, you must learn what cannot yet
be taught here. I may one day need
from you what no one else can give.
At last, Isa nods. Not gladly. But he nods.
Ali places hands on his shoulders, gently kisses his forehead.

EXT. DESERT - DAWN
ISA drives his horse across the sand, trying to outrun his
father’s words. He pulls up on a rise, breathing hard. He turns.
Behind him, another horse and rider approach from the distance.
HAYA (now 18) rides toward him. Her scarf streams on the wind. She
reins in hard beside him. Sharp-eyed, proud, no longer the girl he
remembers — and yet entirely the same.
HAYA
Isa. I missed you at the palace.
Their eyes hold. She senses something is wrong.
ISA
My father wants to send me away, Haya.
To study.
Anger breaks through.
ISA (CONT'D)
As if there is no use for me here.
HAYA
Is that what he said? Or are those
your words?
It stings because it is true.
ISA
(bitter)
My future no longer belongs to me.

HAYA
Your father is not punishing you, Isa.
He sees promise in you. He hopes you
return with purpose.
The sun edges higher. Gold touches their faces. Neither can say
more, though both want to.
ISA
And what do you hope?
Haya lowers her eyes. Too cautious to speak.
A gust lifts Haya’s scarf. Isa catches it instinctively before it
slips away. For a moment, he holds the fabric. As he offers it
back, their fingers brush. A brief accidental touch — gone almost
before it can be acknowledged.
HAYA
Poor Isa. Always rushing to catch life
before it escapes you.
The old phrase sparks between them.
HAYA (CONT'D)
Life is a long road ahead of you. You
must learn to ride more slowly.
ISA
I have never been good at that.
Isa gathers his reins, then hesitates.
ISA (CONT'D)
Haya. If I was away a long time...
would you remember me kindly?
Haya’s composure falters only slightly.
HAYA
Do not ask me such things from a
horse’s back, Isa.
He understands. A sad, private smile passes between them.
ISA
Then someday I will ask better, when I
am standing on the ground.
The tenderness of it unsettles him. He nods. For a moment, neither
moves. Then Isa turns his horse toward the horizon. He rides off
toward the rising sun.
Haya watches him go, tears held back with difficulty.

EXT. DESERT - FIRST LIGHT
Majeed ON HORSEBACK streaks across the wondrous landscape.
SHEIKH ISA (V.O.)
These were difficult lessons for a
young man. It seemed life could not
present any greater burden.

EXT. ROYAL RESIDENCE / COURTYARD - CONTINUOUS
Majeed dismounts with urgency and enters the palace.
SHEIKH ISA (V.O.)
But young men can hold foolish
thoughts. Soon I would learn the
hardest lesson of all.

INT. ROYAL RESIDENCE / BED CHAMBER - CONTINUOUS
Majeed rushes in to wake Ali.
MAJEED
Your Honour, Your Honour.
But Ali is already stirring. Staff accumulate in the hallway.
MAJEED (CONT'D)
Scouts report emissaries from
Zallaq approaching.
SHEIKH ALI
Zallaq? At this hour?

EXT. EAST RIFF'A - DAWN
A cold dawn over the dunes.
SHEIKH ALI & IBRAHIM lead a large force of fifty fighters. Swords.
Rifles.
SALEH, Ali’s advisor, rides at his side.
Across the field waits another force. Fifty strong as well, with
hard-faced MERCENARIES among them. Two FIELD CANNONS.
At their centre: SHEIKH MOHAMMED (60’s).
Beside him: SHEIKH ABDULLAH BIN AHMED AL-KHALIFA (50s).

Ali sees his brother. Then Abdullah. Concern passes across his
face, quickly buried.
SHEIKH ALI
Saleh.
Saleh rides out beneath a white cloth.
IBRAHIM
